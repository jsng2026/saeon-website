CUSHION

Privacy Policy

Last updated 10 September 2026.

Overview

Cushion blocks listed adult websites in Safari and, when Spotcheck is enabled, shares a sample of browsing with a trusted person who agrees to receive it. No account, advertising SDK or analytics SDK is required.

What stays on the device

Cushion stores the trusted person’s name and email, an optional display name, settings, subscription status and pending captures in shared app storage. A capture contains a page address, title, timestamp and random local identifier. Queries and fragments are removed except for recognised search terms on supported search engines. Paths and titles can still contain private or sensitive information. Cushion does not take screenshots.

Pending captures are capped at 500 and are pruned after 30 days while capture processing is active, except for a fixed batch retained for retry. Successful submission removes acknowledged queued data.

What leaves the device

Relevant report samples or alert details, recipient information and the chosen display name travel over HTTPS to Cushion’s delivery relay and then to its email provider, Resend. The provider necessarily receives the destination and message content. The relay holds raw message data in request memory while sending, but does not intentionally store raw email addresses, names, URLs, titles or report bodies in its database.

StoreKit handles purchases; Cushion does not receive payment-card information. Feedback submitted through the app is emailed for support purposes.

Records retained by the relay

  • Random relationship and recipient-action tokens, consent and unsubscribe timestamps, and a hash binding the relationship to its recipient.
  • Hashed-recipient rate counters and delivery-failure status.
  • Submission identifiers, message hashes, leases, acknowledgement times and the last routine-report delivery day, used to prevent duplicates.

These records contain no raw browsing report or raw recipient address. Infrastructure providers may process ordinary operational request metadata.

Consent and stopping reports

A trusted person receives an invitation, and reporting waits for their consent. Opening a confirmation link alone does not record consent; the recipient must press the confirmation button. They can unsubscribe, which stops future submissions for that relationship. A message already handed to the email provider may still arrive.

Timing

Reports are combined into at most one routine submission per delivery day, after the delivery hour and when Safari or Cushion next runs. An inactive device can delay reports. Silence does not prove there was no browsing, that extensions remain enabled, or that the app remains installed. Accountability alerts are separate from routine reports.

Boundaries

Cushion does not sell data, share it with advertisers or monitor browsing outside its Safari extension. It cannot report its own deletion, enforce Safari’s system switches, inspect unknown or in-app browsers, or retract delivered email. It is not directed at children under 13.
